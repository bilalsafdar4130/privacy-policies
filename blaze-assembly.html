Privacy Policy — Blaze Assembly

Last updated: 8 October 2026 · Package com.blazeassembly.app

Blaze Assembly (“the app”, “we”, “us”) is an idle sparkler-art game for Android. This policy explains what information the app collects, why, who it is shared with, and what control you have over it.

The short version.
  • There is no account. You never give us a name, an email address or a phone number, because there is nowhere to type one.
  • The app shows advertising, served by Google AdMob — a banner, and occasionally a full-screen or rewarded video ad. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent form asks you first, and no ad is requested until you have answered.
  • In-app purchases are optional: Remove ads, a single payment, once, and three coin packs, all through Google Play Billing. Nothing playable is ever locked behind them — everything coins buy can also be earned by playing.
  • The app collects anonymous gameplay statistics through Google Firebase Analytics, and nothing else beyond what serving an advertisement or processing a purchase requires.
  • You can switch analytics off at any time, and in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland it is off until you say otherwise.
  • Your saved progress lives on your device. It is never uploaded.
  • Advertising is served by Google AdMob, and may also be served through AppLovin — see Advertising and your ad choices.

What the app collects

Anonymous gameplay statistics (Firebase Analytics)

The app reports events describing what happens in the game — a level opened, a shape finished, a fuse lit, an upgrade bought, a setting changed, or an error — so that we can tell which parts of the game people enjoy and which parts are broken.

What the app does not collect

No name, email address, phone number, postal address, contacts, calendar, photos, files, microphone, camera, precise or approximate location, browsing or search history, list of installed apps, health or fitness data, or payment information — Google Play handles payment details for every purchase in the app, and this app is never told them. There is no chat, no user-generated content and no way for one player to see another.

Advertising

The app shows advertising served by Google AdMob: currently a banner, and the game is also built to show an occasional full-screen or a rewarded video ad. Serving an ad means AdMob reads your device’s advertising ID — a resettable identifier, never joined to your Firebase Analytics identifier or your gameplay statistics.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent form comes first. Google’s EU User Consent Policy requires a certified consent management platform before any ad request from those regions; the app uses Google’s own (the User Messaging Platform), and no ad is requested and the advertising ID is not read until you have answered it. You can change your answer at any time in Settings → About and legal → Data and analytics → Your data and ad choices → Ad privacy settings.

Ads are personalised only where the privacy gate allows it; you can reset the advertising ID, or opt out of personalisation, in your phone’s own settings. Buying Remove ads (see below) turns advertising off entirely, on that device, for good.

In-app purchases

Every purchase is optional and is processed entirely by Google Play Billing. This app never sees your card details, billing address or any other payment information — only which product was bought and whether the purchase succeeded.

  • Remove ads — a single, one-time payment (not a subscription) that switches advertising off for good. Nothing playable is locked behind it: it removes a strip of screen, and that is all it claims to do. If you change devices, Play remembers the purchase and it is restored automatically the next time the app checks.
  • Coin packs (Handful, Sackful and Cartload) — one-off purchases that add in-game coins to your save. Coins are not money: they cannot be cashed out or transferred, and they live in the save file on your device like coins you earn by playing. Everything they buy can also be earned.

When a purchase completes, the app records a purchase event in Firebase Analytics with the product and the price Google Play showed you, so we can tell which products people find worth buying. It is not linked to your name, your Google account or your payment details.

Why we collect it

  • To fix things. The app reports when a save fails or a level cannot be loaded. Those events should be permanently zero; when they are not, they are the only way we find out.
  • To decide what to build. Whether people reach the moment the fuse is lit, and how far they get before they stop, is what tells us which parts of the game are working.
  • To serve and measure advertising where it is shown, and to complete a purchase where one is made.
  • Nothing is used to build a profile of you beyond what serving a non-personalised advertisement requires, or to make any decision about you as an individual. Gameplay analytics is counted, not read.

Who it is shared with

Google processes data on our behalf, as the operator of Firebase Analytics (gameplay statistics), Google AdMob (advertising), and Google Play Billing (purchases). It is not sold, and it is not shared with anyone else — no advertising networks other than Google AdMob and AppLovin (see Advertising and your ad choices), no data brokers, no other analytics providers.

Your choices

Turning it off

Open Settings → About and legal → Data and analytics in the app and switch Anonymous statistics off. Nothing further is collected, from that moment, on that device. The game plays exactly the same either way — no feature is withheld, and nothing is nagged.

Consent in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

If your device looks like it is in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, nothing at all is collected until you agree. The app asks once, on first launch, about statistics, with both answers on the same screen at the same size; saying nothing is the same as saying no. Then Google’s consent form asks about advertising. You can change either answer, in either direction, for ever afterwards, in Settings → About and legal → Data and analytics.

Deleting it

Settings → About and legal → Delete my data discards the anonymous identifier Firebase holds for this installation and the record of your privacy choice. Anything sent before that point cannot be joined to anything sent after it.

Uninstalling the app removes your saved progress from the device entirely.

Your rights

Your legal rights, region by region, and where to complain are set out in Your privacy rights, wherever you live.

Where your progress is kept

Coins, upgrades, unlocked levels, colours and settings are stored in a single file in the app’s own private storage on your device. It is never uploaded, it is not backed up to any server of ours, and no other app can read it. Android’s own backup for this app is switched off (user_data_backup/allow=false).

Security

Everything the app sends is encrypted in transit (HTTPS, by the Firebase, AdMob and Google Play Billing SDKs). The app requests these permissions: INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (so the analytics, advertising and purchase SDKs can reach the network), VIBRATE (the haptic tick when a sparkler is planted), AD_ID (the advertising ID, for advertising) and com.android.vending.BILLING (Google Play purchases). It requests no location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage, photo or calendar permission.

Advertising and your ad choices

Blaze Assembly shows advertising to stay free to play. This section applies to every advertisement in the app, from every network.

How it works:

  • Who serves the ads. Google AdMob, which may also fill an ad slot through mediation with AppLovin (AppLovin MAX) and the ad networks they work with. Ad content is limited to a general-audience rating.
  • What an ad network receives. Your device's advertising ID (a resettable identifier Android provides; the app declares the AD_ID permission for it), your IP address (from which the network infers an approximate location such as your country or city), technical information about your device and the app, and how each ad performed — whether it loaded, was shown or was tapped. We never give an ad network your name, email address, contacts, precise location or anything you type.
  • The advertising ID is kept apart from analytics. It is read for advertising only and is never joined to the app's own statistics.
  • Europe, the UK and Switzerland: consent first. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland the app shows Google's consent form (Google's User Messaging Platform, a Google-certified consent management platform that uses the IAB Transparency and Consent Framework) before any ad is requested. No ad is requested and the advertising ID is not read until you have answered. Ads are personalised only if you agree to personalisation; otherwise any ad you see is non-personalised, chosen from the app and the screen it appears on.
  • United States: opt out of targeted advertising. Where a US state privacy law gives you the right to opt out of the “sale” or “sharing” of personal information or of targeted advertising, Google's privacy form offers that choice; see US state privacy notice.
  • Change your answer in the app at any time: Settings → About and legal → Data and analytics → Your data and ad choices → Ad privacy settings reopens Google's privacy form. It is shown wherever Google's form applies to you.
  • Anywhere in the world you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of ads personalisation, in your phone's own settings (usually Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Settings → Google → Ads). Ads still appear, but they are not based on a profile of you.
  • Remove ads. The Remove ads purchase switches advertising off for good, on every network, and Google Play restores it if you reinstall on the same Google account.
  • Children. No personalised advertising is shown to anyone we know to be under 13.

Each network uses the information under its own privacy policy: How Google uses information from apps that use its services · Google Privacy Policy · AppLovin Privacy Policy.

How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you delete it in the app or uninstall the app.
  • Firebase Analytics event data: 14 months, then Google deletes it automatically. Aggregate reports with no identifier attached may be kept longer.
  • Advertising data is kept by each ad network under its own retention policy. Google, for example, anonymises advertising data in its logs by removing part of the IP address after 9 months and cookie information after 18 months.
  • Emails you send us are kept only as long as we need them to answer you, and deleted on request.

Delete your data

In the app: open Settings → About and legal → Privacy policy and tap Delete all my data. It erases your progress and coins, throws away the anonymous analytics identifier and resets your privacy and advertising choices, on the spot. (Delete my data further down the same screen removes only the analytics identifier and keeps your progress.)

What it cannot delete: the Remove ads purchase is held by Google Play against your Google account and stays yours. Coins from coin packs are part of your progress and are erased with it; Google Play keeps its own record of the payment, which you can see in your Play account. Analytics already sent is deleted by Google after 14 months, or earlier on request by email.

By email: if you cannot open the app, or you want Google's analytics copy removed as well, email bilalsafdar3014@gmail.com with the subject “Blaze Assembly data deletion”. We answer within 30 days. We will never ask for a password.

Uninstalling the app also deletes everything it stored on your device.
